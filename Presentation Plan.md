# Hakbang: Final Presentation Plan

25 minutes total: **15-minute pitch** (how we sell Hakbang) + **10-minute MVP demo** (what we're selling).
Every number here comes from `Business Plan.md`, with the chapter it's from.

---

## The one-sentence story

> Graduates can't get hired without experience, and companies want to train but can't find the right people to train. Hakbang connects them: free training from a verified employer, and a real job at the end. Employers pay only when someone gets hired. With ₱2M we take this from a pilot to about 1,900 formal jobs a year.

Every slide should move this sentence forward. If a slide doesn't, cut it.

**Plan for about 11 slides.** Put one idea and one big number on each. Speakers should never read a sentence off the slide.

### Who speaks

Keep handoffs to four or fewer in 15 minutes. One way to split it:

- **Miguel (CEO):** beats 1 and 10. Opens and closes, so the story stays with one voice.
- **Reinman (COO):** beats 2 and 3. He ran the customer interviews, so the problem is his to tell.
- **Francine (CFO):** beats 5, 7, and 9. All the money: the fee, break-even, and the ask.
- **Jaztin (CMO):** beats 4, 6, and 8. The product promise, the market, and the impact.
- **Hyun (CTO):** drives the demo (Part 2).

### Mistakes to avoid

- **Don't use the SOM revenue figures from Ch. III (₱1.6M in Year 1).** Ch. IX gives **₱1.38M** because it includes the cheaper pilot rate. If you need a revenue number, use Ch. IX, but the pitch works without one.
- **Don't spend time on Year 2's 2% margin of safety,** but have an answer ready if someone asks (see Q&A below).
- **Don't suggest Hakbang alone will lower the national unemployment rate.** Say instead that it adds formal jobs one hire at a time, in a model that can scale.

## Part 2: The MVP demo (10:00)

**Format: a live role-play, not a walkthrough.** Two presenters play the two sides and talk to each other, while a narrator connects each screen back to the pitch. The audience follows a person instead of a list of features.

- **Paolo** (job seeker) on a phone, or with the browser in mobile view, mirrored to the screen.
- **Carla** (BlueHarbor's training lead) on the laptop, in the employer dashboard.
- **Narrator** (Hyun) introduces each moment in one line and points to what matters.

Use the built-in presenter guide (the **Compass** button, or add `?guide` to the URL). Its 8 pitch steps match this script. Open with `?fresh` to reset the demo data before you start.

| Time | Moment | Who | What happens | The line that lands it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0:00–0:45 | Setup | Narrator | Landing page. "You met Paolo in the pitch. Here he is, on his phone, on day one." | "Everything you're about to see is the actual product, not slides." |
| 0:45–2:30 | **Sign up and the AI profile** | Paolo | Sign up → privacy notice (RA 10173) → upload a resume → AI fills in the Living Profile → Paolo corrects a field → saves. | "He spent 8 months filling out applications. This took 30 seconds, and he still checks every field." |

| 4:00–5:00 | **Carla accepts** | Carla | Candidates ranked by skills, location, and schedule only. Accept Paolo. Back on Paolo's phone, his track changes to "In track." | "We don't rank people by their school, only by skills, location, and schedule." |

Then go to Billing: **₱2,500** at the pilot rate. | "Both sides have to confirm. Only now does anyone pay. That's our whole business model on one screen." |
| 7:45–9:00 | **Trust** | Narrator, as Admin | Verification queue: BIR 2303, SEC registration, business permit, and track terms. Approve BlueHarbor, and Carla's "Post a track" unlocks. | "This is why Paolo could trust BlueHarbor in the first place." |
| 9:00–10:00 | Wrap and buffer | Narrator | Back to the landing page. Recap the three promises in one line each. Hand over to Q&A. | "Verified. Trained. Hired." |

### Ways to keep it engaging

- **Two screens, two people.** When Carla clicks Accept and Paolo's phone updates, the room sees both sides of the market working.
- **One question for the audience early on:** "Which track would you pick?" Then take the one the room picks, or the 86% match.
- **Pause at the climax.** After Paolo confirms the contract, stay quiet for a beat before switching to Billing.
- **Don't read the screen.** Say what it *means*. The audience can read the labels themselves.

### Things that will go wrong, and what to do

- **No internet:** run a local build (`npm run build && npm run preview`) on the presenting laptop, and keep a 10-minute screen recording of the full run as a fallback.
- **Demo state gets messed up:** add `?fresh` to reset. Rehearse the reset once.
- **Running over time:** skip the training moment (Fast-forward covers it), then the trust moment. Never skip the hire and billing moment.

## Q&A prep (likely questions)

| Question | Short answer | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What stops a BPO from hiring trainees directly and skipping our fee? | The partner agreement includes the hiring commitment and the fee. Hires are confirmed in the dashboard by both sides, and we follow each hire for 90 days. | Ch. II B.7 |
| Does Hakbang need a DOLE recruitment license? | We'll ask DOLE before the pilot. ₱100,000 of the seed round is set aside for fees, or we partner with a licensed agency. | Ch. VIII A, Ch. X D |
| Year 2 breaks even with only 2% to spare. Isn't that risky? | Yes, so we track the 78-hires-a-month line every month. If we miss it two months in a row, the July 2028 hires are delayed. The 50% reserve covers slow months. | Ch. IX E |
| What if AI replaces entry-level BPO jobs? | We add tracks for AI-assisted roles and expand to retail, logistics, and MSME employers. | Ch. VIII A |
| What if a big job board copies us? | Our partner relationships and record of verified hires are hard to copy, and we publish results every quarter. | Ch. VIII A |
| What happens after you graduate? | Founder shares vest over four years. Stipends start in Year 2 and salaries in Year 3, and each role has written handover notes. | Ch. VIII A |
